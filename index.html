Before you check the speed

First check whether a worker is actively stopping traffic with a STOP/SLOW paddle. If so, this is likely a flagging setup with a 50 to 100 ft taper. If traffic is merging around a closed lane instead, continue below.

Measure by sight

One lane-line cycle is about 40 ft.

A typical broken white lane line has a 10 ft painted line followed by a 30 ft gap. Count the line plus the following gap along the cone taper.

10 ft line + 30 ft gap = 40 ft cycle

What to watch for

A good taper makes the merge feel natural.

Temporary traffic control should make the next move obvious before drivers reach the cones. With enough taper length, traffic has room to naturally zipper merge. When a setup skips that distance or uses only a few cones, the merge can feel sudden instead of guided.

Red flags from a driver's view

These signs do not prove a violation by themselves, but they can mean the setup deserves a closer safety review.

  • The lane closes suddenly.
  • Cones force an abrupt merge.
  • Drivers brake hard or swerve.
  • The taper is only a few cones long on a faster road.

Louisville reporting

Report a potentially unsafe lane closure.

If a taper looks dramatically shorter than the benchmark, report the location, direction of travel, speed limit, whether anyone was stopping traffic with a paddle, and whether photos or dashcam video are available.

Kentucky state-maintained routes may use KYTC standard drawings with longer taper expectations. For state routes or highways, KYTC may be the right agency to contact.

Why it exists

TaperCheck turns standards into a quick public safety reference.

The goal is not to attack workers in the field. The goal is better accountability, training, planning, and oversight from the companies and agencies responsible for temporary traffic control.

TaperCheck is an educational estimate based on standard merge taper-length logic. It is not an official engineering determination.
